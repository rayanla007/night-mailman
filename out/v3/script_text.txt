This town loses gravity for one minute every day. At exactly twelve o'clock. A siren goes off, and everyone grabs the nearest railing. Because if you don't... you float. Cars float. Dogs float. Even the mayor's toupee floats. Sixty seconds later, everything drops. We call it Float Hour. Even though it's a minute. I'm Ivy. This is my little brother, Bo. Bo has never held a railing in his life. He says floating is the best part of the day. Our grandpa Gus winds the giant town clock every morning. He told us one rule. "If the clock ever stops... hold on, and don't let go." Bo laughed. Then came Tuesday. 11:59. The siren. Everyone grabbed a railing. Bo grabbed his balloon. Twelve o'clock. We floated. Ten seconds. Thirty. Sixty. Nothing came down. The clock had stopped. And everything kept going up. Cars. Mailboxes. The school bus. The fountain water floated up in giant bubbles. Bo waved at me... and kept rising. I screamed his name. Grandpa yelled, "The key! Someone stole the key!" Without the key, the clock can't be wound. And without the clock, there's no gravity at all. The whole town was drifting into the clouds. That's when I saw him. Mayor Grimsby. Holding the key. "No more Float Hour," he said. "From now on... it's Float Forever!" His new sky hotel opens tomorrow. So I let go. I pushed off a lamppost and flew straight at him. Biscuit flew with me. Biscuit bit the toupee. The mayor dropped the key. I caught it. Gravity: three percent. I grabbed Bo's shoelace on the way up. Grandpa held the clock door open. The keyhole was taller than me. We all pushed. Once. Twice. Three times. The gears roared back to life. Tick. Everything came down. Really fast. The mayor landed in the fountain. His toupee landed on Biscuit. Biscuit refuses to give it back. Now the key hangs around Grandpa's neck. The mayor? He polishes railings now. And Bo holds a railing. Mostly. Everything was normal again. But last night, I woke up at 3 a.m. My bed was touching the ceiling. And the clock tower said twelve o'clock. Someone else has a key. Hold on.
