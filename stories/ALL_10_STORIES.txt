==================== BATTERY TOWN ====================

In this town, everyone has a battery bar floating over their head.
When it's full, you're happy.
When it's empty, you fall asleep wherever you are.
In the middle of the street.
In the middle of a sentence.
Everyone charges at night, in giant charging stations.
Plug in. Sleep. Wake up at one hundred percent.
Everyone except you.
Your battery has never moved.
It's been stuck at one percent your whole life.
Doctors can't explain it.
The charging station won't even let you in.
"Error. Battery not found."
So you just... stay awake.
You're the only one who has ever seen the town at night.
Empty streets.
Every house dark.
Every person plugged in.
But last night, the charging station made a sound.
Something it has never done before.
A voice.
"Charging complete. Begin harvest."
All the batteries in town started draining.
One hundred percent. Seventy. Forty.
Not into the people.
Out of them.
Into the station.
You ran to your mom's charging pod.
Her battery was at twelve percent.
You pulled her plug.
Alarms went off everywhere.
And every head in town turned toward you.
Their eyes opened.
All at the same time.
"Battery not found," they said.
"Battery not found."
That's when you realized.
Your battery isn't broken.
It's the only one they can't eat.

==================== QUIET LAW ====================

In this town, you only get one hundred words per day.
Everyone has a counter on their chest.
Every word you say, it goes down.
When it hits zero, your voice is gone until tomorrow.
So nobody wastes words here.
No small talk.
No "how are you."
Just nods.
My little sister Pia spends her words on singing.
Every morning. All one hundred of them.
Then she's quiet for the rest of the day.
Dad says she's wasting them.
Pia says songs are the only words worth saying.
Then the mayor announced a new rule.
Words don't reset anymore.
Whatever you have left... is all you'll ever get.
The whole town went silent.
People started hoarding words.
Selling them.
Stealing them.
I had forty-two left.
Pia had zero.
She had already sung that morning.
She would never speak again.
She just looked at me and smiled.
That night, I found the mayor's office.
A giant machine.
Full of words.
Millions of them.
Everyone's words.
He wasn't saving them.
He was collecting them.
For one giant speech.
One that would make everyone in town obey him forever.
I had forty-two words.
I needed exactly one.
I pressed the microphone.
And I sang.
The machine cracked.
Words flew out like birds.
Back to everyone.
Pia opened her mouth.
And she sang the whole morning song.
My counter says zero now.
I don't mind.
She talks enough for both of us.

==================== MIRROR HOUR ====================

In this town, reflections go home at three o'clock.
Every mirror goes blank.
Every window. Every puddle.
For one hour, nobody has a reflection.
Your reflection has its own house, its own job, its own friends.
They come back at four. Every single day.
You've never thought about it.
Until today.
At four o'clock, everybody's reflection came back.
Except yours.
You waved at the mirror.
Nothing waved back.
Your mom looked at the empty mirror.
Then at you.
And slowly took a step back.
Because in this town, there's a saying.
If your reflection doesn't come back...
it found someone it likes better.
You started searching.
The mirror maze at the fair.
The lake.
The shop windows on Main Street.
Nothing.
Then you saw it.
In the window of the house across the street.
Your reflection.
Sitting at a dinner table.
Laughing.
With a family you've never met.
It looked happy.
Happier than you've ever looked.
It saw you.
It stopped laughing.
And it walked to the window.
It pressed its hand against the glass.
And mouthed three words.
"Your turn now."
Then the glass went soft.
Like water.
And it pulled.

==================== WEATHER REMOTE ====================

In this town, the weather has a remote control.
And only the mayor is allowed to touch it.
Sunny for his birthday.
Snow on Christmas.
Rain on the day of the school trip. Every year.
Nobody knows why he hates school trips.
My best friend Kai says the remote is just a myth.
So we snuck into the town hall to find it.
It was real.
Big. Silver. One hundred buttons.
Buttons like "light breeze."
"Rainbow."
"Fog. Extra thick."
And one red button covered in tape.
Kai pressed "snow."
In July.
Outside, kids screamed with joy.
The whole town turned white.
Then Kai got curious.
Tornado.
Just a small one.
It was not small.
It picked up the bakery.
Then the school bus.
Then the mayor.
We had to fix it.
I pressed "calm."
Nothing.
"Sunny."
Nothing.
The batteries were dying.
Only one button still had power.
The red one.
Under the tape, it said:
"Do not press. Ever."
Kai looked at me.
The tornado was coming straight for us.
I pressed it.
Everything stopped.
The tornado.
The wind.
The birds.
The people.
Everything except me and Kai.
The red button doesn't control the weather.
It pauses the world.
And there is no unpause button.

==================== FLOOR ZERO ====================

In this town, everyone lives in one building.
One hundred floors.
The richer you are, the higher you live.
I live on floor two.
Just above the parking garage.
Every elevator in the building has the same buttons.
One to one hundred.
Every elevator except one.
The old one, at the end of the hall.
It has a button nobody talks about.
Zero.
Grandma says it's broken.
Grandma also says never press it.
So obviously, I pressed it.
The doors closed.
The lights flickered.
And the elevator went down.
For a very long time.
When the doors opened, I saw a lobby.
Exactly like ours.
Same plants.
Same carpet.
Same people.
But everyone was standing still.
Staring at the elevator.
Waiting.
A man in a suit walked up to me.
He looked exactly like the building manager.
But his smile was too wide.
"Welcome to floor zero," he said.
"We've been waiting for a volunteer."
"Volunteer for what?" I asked.
"For the swap. One of us goes up. One of you stays down."
Behind him, a girl stepped forward.
She had my hair.
My hoodie.
My face.
I ran back into the elevator.
I pressed two.
Two. Two. Two.
The doors closed just in time.
At least I think they did.
Because this morning, Grandma made my favorite breakfast.
And I didn't like it.

==================== PET RAIN ====================

In this town, once a year, it rains pets.
Not cats and dogs.
Well, yes, cats and dogs.
But also hamsters. Parrots. Goldfish.
One year, a llama.
Everyone runs outside with nets and umbrellas.
Whatever lands in your yard is yours.
That's the rule.
This year, I was ready.
Biggest net in town.
Blanket on the grass.
I wanted a puppy.
The clouds turned pink.
The first pets started falling.
Puppies.
Kittens.
A very confused turtle.
My neighbor caught a golden retriever.
My best friend caught twin bunnies.
I caught nothing.
Not one single pet.
The sky went quiet.
Everyone went home happy.
Then I heard it.
A tiny squeak.
Under my net.
A puppy.
Smallest I've ever seen.
I called him Pebble.
But Pebble didn't eat dog food.
Pebble didn't bark.
And every night, Pebble grew.
One week later, Pebble was as big as the house.
The mayor called an emergency meeting.
Pebble wasn't a puppy.
Pebble was a baby cloud.
And somewhere up there...
his mom was looking for him.
That's why this year...
it rained for forty days.

==================== SHADOW SHIFT ====================

In this town, shadows work the night shift.
When we go to sleep, our shadows get up.
They fix the roads.
Clean the streets.
Paint the fences.
That's why our town is always perfect in the morning.
Nobody has ever seen them work.
You're not allowed to look.
Not even once.
But I couldn't sleep.
So I looked out my window.
Hundreds of shadows were working on Main Street.
Tall ones. Small ones.
Mine was there too.
I recognized its messy hair.
It was carrying a ladder.
Then it stopped.
And turned around.
And looked straight at my window.
I hid under my blanket.
In the morning, everything was normal.
Except my shadow was gone.
I stood in the sun.
Nothing on the ground.
I asked my friend Leo.
His shadow was fine.
At school, my teacher pointed at the floor where my shadow should be.
And went very pale.
"It quit," she whispered.
"Shadows only quit when they find a better person."
That night, I followed the shadows.
To the old factory.
Where they sleep during the day.
And there it was. My shadow.
Attached to someone else.
A boy who looked just like me.
Same hoodie. Same messy hair.
He smiled at me.
"Thanks for the shadow," he said.
"Now I just need the rest of you."

==================== NAME SWAP ====================

In this town, every year, everyone swaps names.
At midnight, on New Year's, your name tag changes.
And whatever name you get...
that's who you are now.
Your job. Your house. Your family.
Last year, the baker became a firefighter.
The firefighter became the mayor.
The mayor became a baby.
It's chaos. But it's tradition.
Everyone wears their name tag at all times.
This year, I got a name nobody had ever heard of.
Nobody in town had that name.
No house. No job. No family.
The mayor checked the town records.
"This name doesn't exist."
So I went looking.
The old library.
The town archive.
The cemetery.
Nothing.
Then I found it.
Carved under the town clock tower.
One name.
Mine.
And a date.
Exactly one hundred years ago.
Under it, a little door.
I opened it.
Inside, there was a dusty room.
A bed.
A desk.
And a kid sitting there.
Waiting.
He looked up at me.
"Finally," he said.
"I've been wearing your name for a hundred years."
He pointed at my chest.
My name tag was changing.
Letter by letter.
To his name.

==================== UPSIDE DOWN FRIDAY ====================

In this town, every Friday, the world flips upside down.
At exactly three o'clock, gravity goes the other way.
Everyone grabs the handles on the ceiling.
Houses have doors on the roof.
Trees grow both ways.
Pizza is always eaten upside down.
For one hour, the sky is the floor.
Then at four, everything flips back.
Easy.
My little brother Milo loves Flip Friday.
He keeps his shoes on the ceiling just for fun.
But this Friday, something was wrong.
At three o'clock, the world flipped.
At four o'clock... it didn't flip back.
Five o'clock.
Six o'clock.
Midnight.
People started living on the ceiling.
Sleeping on the ceiling.
Cooking on the ceiling.
Milo was thrilled.
I was not.
Because I noticed something.
Every hour, the sky below us got a little closer.
The clouds were reaching up.
Grabbing.
Pulling.
The town was falling.
Into the sky.
I found the flip switch inside the old windmill.
It was stuck.
Someone had jammed it with a shoe.
A tiny shoe.
Milo's shoe.
"I just wanted it to last longer," he said.
I pulled the shoe out.
The world flipped back.
Everyone hit the ground.
Every Friday since, Milo keeps both shoes on.
And the sky still looks at us...
like it's hungry.

==================== LOST AND FOUND ====================

In this town, nothing stays lost.
Everything you lose ends up in one place.
The Lost and Found.
A huge building in the center of town.
Lost keys. Lost socks. Lost homework.
Even lost dogs.
You just walk in, find your stuff, and walk out.
Mister Finch runs it.
He has worked there for sixty years.
He knows where everything is.
Last week, I lost my backpack.
So I went to the Lost and Found.
Aisle twelve. Backpacks.
Right next to aisle thirteen.
Lost time.
Jars full of glowing minutes.
"Lost when you were bored," the labels said.
Aisle fourteen.
Lost memories.
Aisle fifteen.
Lost voices.
And at the very end...
aisle sixteen.
Lost people.
Shelves of sleeping kids.
Hundreds of them.
Each with a tag on their wrist.
One of them was my brother.
Except my brother is at home.
Eating dinner.
Right now.
I ran to Mister Finch.
"Who's that at my house?" I asked.
He smiled.
"Whatever the town finds..." he said.
"it gives back."
"Even if it isn't the same."
That night, my brother asked me where I'd been.
I told him the Lost and Found.
He stopped smiling.
"Don't go back there," he whispered.
"They're running out of shelves."
