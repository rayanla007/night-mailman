In this town, every year, everyone swaps names.
At midnight, on New Year's, your name tag changes.
And whatever name you get...
that's who you are now.
Your job. Your house. Your family.
Last year, the baker became a firefighter.
The firefighter became the mayor.
The mayor became a baby.
It's chaos. But it's tradition.
Everyone wears their name tag at all times.
This year, I got a name nobody had ever heard of.
Nobody in town had that name.
No house. No job. No family.
The mayor checked the town records.
"This name doesn't exist."
So I went looking.
The old library.
The town archive.
The cemetery.
Nothing.
Then I found it.
Carved under the town clock tower.
One name.
Mine.
And a date.
Exactly one hundred years ago.
Under it, a little door.
I opened it.
Inside, there was a dusty room.
A bed.
A desk.
And a kid sitting there.
Waiting.
He looked up at me.
"Finally," he said.
"I've been wearing your name for a hundred years."
He pointed at my chest.
My name tag was changing.
Letter by letter.
To his name.
