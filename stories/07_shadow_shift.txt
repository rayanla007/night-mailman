In this town, shadows work the night shift.
When we go to sleep, our shadows get up.
They fix the roads.
Clean the streets.
Paint the fences.
That's why our town is always perfect in the morning.
Nobody has ever seen them work.
You're not allowed to look.
Not even once.
But I couldn't sleep.
So I looked out my window.
Hundreds of shadows were working on Main Street.
Tall ones. Small ones.
Mine was there too.
I recognized its messy hair.
It was carrying a ladder.
Then it stopped.
And turned around.
And looked straight at my window.
I hid under my blanket.
In the morning, everything was normal.
Except my shadow was gone.
I stood in the sun.
Nothing on the ground.
I asked my friend Leo.
His shadow was fine.
At school, my teacher pointed at the floor where my shadow should be.
And went very pale.
"It quit," she whispered.
"Shadows only quit when they find a better person."
That night, I followed the shadows.
To the old factory.
Where they sleep during the day.
And there it was. My shadow.
Attached to someone else.
A boy who looked just like me.
Same hoodie. Same messy hair.
He smiled at me.
"Thanks for the shadow," he said.
"Now I just need the rest of you."
